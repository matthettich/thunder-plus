# U30 notes: the ...waves engine in the core

Branch `n3/u30`, worktree work in `native/` and `docs/port/` only. Source of truth: `waves/waves-worklet.js` plus what `index.html` embeds (`wavesPre`, `wavesLiveRender`).

## What is in it
- `native/core/src/waves/waves.h` the API; `wtable.inc` (generated by `native/tools/waves-gen.mjs`, `--check` for drift): ports, params and flags of all 130 module types.
- `wmods.cpp`: the waves-only modules (HostIn, VCO, FM, Additive, Noise, Plaits, Elements, VCF, VCA, Mixer, LFO, ADSR, Random, Atten, Sequencer, Out, KString/KSString, Scope, Expression, Pitch delay, Pitch reverb), the 33 track-effect wrappers (`Eff`, with the page's maps and `wetAt`), the registry.
- `engine.cpp`: the graph (Map insertion order, Kahn sort with leftovers appended, feedback cables, tails/exits, per-op compile as the JS does it, `_gather`, `_render`), VoicePool (steal order, setOnly, `_cull`), `loadPatch`, transport, handling of the page's messages.
- `live.cpp`: `WavesLive` (the live runner), `renderOffline`, `waves_pre_render`, `waves_live_render`.
- `modfx` additive edits: `kMaxPorts` 12 to 20 (the Mixer has 16 inputs); `Module::arm(Seeds&)` (Filter, Sample and hold, BBD, Grain delay, Ring mod, Spring, Chaos, Marbles override it).
- Reference: `native/tools/waves-dump.mjs` (Node; also runs the page's own `wavesPre` / `wavesLiveRender`) writes `native/tests/golden/waves_cases.json` (666 cases) and `waves_render.json` (30 page-render cases). Nothing is hand-edited. `waves_effects.json` is the per-case record of the 198 library cases (RMS and length of the web's output, exact or the figure of those beyond -100 dB), made by `test_waves --ref <dump> --write-effects` from the reference, never typed.
- Tests: `test_waves` (golden runner + behaviours), `test_waves_fuzz`, `rt_harness` case `run_waves()` (allocation guard), `native/tools/mutate-u30.sh <builddir>` (16 mutations).

## The page's live protocol (what the runner reproduces)
The page's live node (`wavesLiveRender` / the lane code in `index.html`) is driven by lanes: `r` (row: note, vel, tune), `g` (gate), `t` (tempo in BPM), `l` (level dB / drive / soft / bus) and `v` (voice settings). A note lands at the start of the host block in which it arrives; legato inside a lane moves the lane's voice to the new pitch without a new gate; a stolen voice restarts its lane row; pitch follow is applied once per block from the lane's rate at the end of the block. The output stage is level (dB), drive (tanh), soft clip, then the bus selected by `setOnly`.

## Where the page's own live output differs from its render (and from this runner)
- The page quantises a note landing to the block start (up to 127 samples late); its render lands it at the sample. This runner lands every event at its sample.
- The page moves a lane's pitch once per block; this runner moves it at the event.
- The page skips a quiet node (idle-skip after 2 s) and sheds load; neither is carried. Scope show() and telemetry are not ported.
- The runner is delayed 127 samples (see choice 2) and its output equals `renderOffline` delayed by 127.

## Live design (sample-exact events, no allocation after prepare)
- Events: `LiveEvent` (NoteOn, NoteOff, Pitch, Tempo, Level, Drive, Soft, Bus, Patch, and the rest of the page's messages) with a sample time. SPSC queue (4096) from the control thread; the audio thread moves due events into a stable sorted pending array (4096).
- Sample exact: a graph block is cut at every pending event time. A block renders when `end <= hostEnd`, `end = min(pos + 128, next event time)`. Because events cut blocks the audio output is independent of the host's block size. Output is delayed 127 samples (the U26 approach), so a block of up to 128 is always complete before it is needed. Choice: the delay is 127 samples (about 2.9 ms at 44.1k). The alternative (no delay, cut at the host boundary) would make the output depend on the host block size.
- Patch handover: `preparePatch()` (control thread) builds the whole patch (modules, buffers, slots) and returns a raw pointer in one of 8 mailbox slots; a `Patch` event names the slot. The audio thread swaps the pointer at the event's sample, hands the old patch back through one of 8 retired slots (`letGo()` runs destroy hooks only, no free), and the control thread frees it with `collectRetired()` / `waves_free_patch`. A refused patch leaves the old one running.
- Exceptions (named): Marbles remakes its state at `arm()` with its seed (one allocation on the audio thread, at the block that first runs it); Expression compiles its text on the audio thread.

## Choices for the lead to confirm
1. States are made at patch preparation (off the audio thread) and armed (seed drawn) at the first block they run, in the JS order; Marbles remakes its state with its seed at arm (the one allocation).
2. Live runner: 127-sample latency plus a cut at every event; events as `LiveEvent`; patch handover by mailbox slots and retired slots, old patch freed on the control thread.
3. No load shedding and no idle-skip (fixed quality).
4. Limits and refusals: sample rate 8000..384000; frames 0..128; maxVoices 0..64; modules at most 1024, cables at most 16384; instances at most 4096, weight at most 4096; ids with '#' or non-string, non-array from/to, null module refuse the patch (the old patch stays); port names that are Object prototype properties are unknown. Every module param is held to its declared range (NaN takes the default), Plaits/Elements notes to +-200 with an engine/model range check, effect map outputs made finite and held to +-1e7, tempo to +-1e5 BPM. These deviate from the JS only for out-of-range inputs; in-range values are bit-exact.
5. Scope show() and telemetry are not ported.
6. Expression compiles on the audio thread (named exception).
7. The `effect()` "bad output" branch is dead (the map cache is skipped).
8. `Host` holds the per-scope seed, random, wasi, stm and daisy state.
9. The Marbles/Tides -100 dB rule, and the 76 listed inexact cases (library-build class; pshift about -55 dB worst).
10. The "factory" patches are factory-like patches I wrote; the repo has no preset files.
11. `loadPatch` compiles per op, faithfully (so it is slow for a big patch but exact).
12. Weight and instance budget numbers (4096 each).
13. maxVoices 0 means `on()` is refused.
14. Non-numeric transport and event values are dropped.
15. `preparePatch` returns a raw pointer with explicit ownership (`swapPatch`, `waves_free_patch`), because PatchImpl is private to the engine.
16. Space (Cloud Seed): the host build sizes its buffers for 96 kHz (`third_party/prepare.sh`: late lines and multitap 131072 floats, allpass stages 4800; the web's tfx/build.sh uses 32768 and 2400) so no tap reads outside its own buffer at 96 kHz, and above 96 kHz space is bypassed in every build. 44.1/48 kHz output is unchanged (every pinned and golden row there is the same before and after; only the two 96 kHz space cases moved). The two 96 kHz space cases are left out by name ("the web's Cloud Seed reads outside its 48 kHz buffers at this rate"). Web finding: the web reads outside Cloud Seed's buffers at 96 kHz (and at 48 kHz with the size control above about .9); nothing changed on the web. Tape: its flutter delay is scaled by the rate and overruns its 500-sample buffer above about 157 kHz, so tape is bypassed above 96 kHz in every build.
17. (withdrawn) The upstream table over-reads at a parameter of exactly 1.0 (Rings resonator.cc 57 and 60, Clouds granular_processor.cc 278-279, Warps modulator.cc 167-168 and 362-363) are patched in `third_party/prepare.sh` with `InterpolateTop` (returns table[size] without the read), host builds only; no output moved. No behaviour exists only in sanitizer builds.

## Memory safety
Hostile patches are reproduced or refused, never a crash, hang or unbounded allocation. `test_waves_fuzz [runs] [seed] [limit s]` builds random hostile patches and event streams (NaN/inf values, huge counts, bad ids, extreme rates and tempos), renders them with a per-render time limit and checks outputs are finite. Found and fixed through it: out-of-range params in Elements/Plaits/tfx/modfx modules (pinned), a dangling `Seeds&` in ChanceGate (persistent `WSeeds`), hostile tempo into the beat clock (step gate index overflow), the upstream table over-reads above.
